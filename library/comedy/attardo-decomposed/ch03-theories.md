# Chapter 3: Theories of Humor and Their Levels

## The three levels of humor analysis (Attardo's framework)

| Level | What it studies | Our equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval** | How humor is accessed from memory | (not our focus) |
| **Comprehension** | How humor is understood structurally | agent gates (slop/edgy/feel) |
| **Appreciation** | How humor is experienced as funny | audience response, laugh |

Attardo argues most humor theories operate at ONE level. The three classical theories each capture a different level:

## The three classical theories

### 1. Release Theory (Freud, Spencer, Bergson)
- **Claim:** Laughter discharges accumulated nervous energy
- **Freud:** Jokes are pressure valves for repressed impulses (the "economy" of psychic energy)
- **Spencer:** Laughter is the sudden relief from restraint
- **Bergson:** "The mechanical encrusted on the living" — rigid systems colliding with organic life
- **Maps to our:** pause-before-punch (tension buildup → release), the hold-then-snap trajectory
- **Testable as:** speech_rate decrease + pause increase before punchline (we measured this: H1, H3)

### 2. Incongruity Theory (Kant, Schopenhauer, Morreall)
- **Claim:** Humor arises when perception violates expectation
- **Kant:** "Laughter is an affection arising from the sudden transformation of a strained expectation into nothing"
- **Schopenhauer:** Humor from the mismatch between concept and real object
- **Morreall:** Humor = pleasant psychological shift from incongruity to resolution
- **Maps to our:** punch surprise, garden_path, semantic_distance, DPV framework
- **Testable as:** punchline_surprisal (-log P(punchline|setup)), setup_uncertainty, semantic_distance

### 3. Aggression Theory (Hobbes, Plato, Gruner)
- **Claim:** Laughter at others' misfortune — their loss is our gain
- **Hobbes:** "Sudden glory" at others' inferiority
- **Gruner:** Comedy as competitive combat
- **Maps to our:** status_invert, target selection, superiority
- **Testable as:** status_delta, target_is_performer, benign_distance

## Attardo's key insight: complementarity

These are NOT competing theories. They describe different aspects:
- Release = the **mechanism** (why the body laughs)
- Incongruity = the **structure** (how the joke is built)
- Aggression = the **social function** (who benefits)

A complete account needs all three. This maps to our multi-layer BV model:
- Violation = incongruity (the structural break)
- Benign = release + distance (the safety that makes it laughable)
- Social target = aggression (who the joke is about)

## Bisociation (Koestler)

Attardo discusses Koestler's bisociation theory: humor arises from the simultaneous perception of an event in two incompatible frames. This is the intellectual ancestor of:
- Our `double_interpret` operator (two readings)
- BV's "simultaneous appraisal" (wrong AND okay at once)
- The GTVH's Script Opposition
