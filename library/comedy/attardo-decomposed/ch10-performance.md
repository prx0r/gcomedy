# Chapter 10: The Performance of Humor

## Why performance matters

Attardo argues humor is not just TEXT — it's PERFORMANCE. The same text delivered differently produces different results. Performance includes:
- Prosody (intonation, stress, rhythm)
- Timing (pauses, pace)
- Gesture (body language)
- Audience interaction

**Our mapping:** delivery + timing + pause + the audience loop

## Timing (Attardo's treatment)

Attardo discusses timing as:
- **Pause distribution:** where silences fall relative to punchlines
- **Speech rate:** how fast the comedian talks
- **Interaction with laughter:** when to resume after audience laughs

**Key finding:** punchlines are preceded by substantial pauses (0.6s+). This is the pause-before-punch we measured (H1: 14x baseline).

**Attardo cites Norrick:** punchlines should be delivered FASTER than setup ("more rapid, fluid delivery"). This is our H3 finding (HOLD then SNAP).

## Prosody and intonation

Attardo discusses how prosody carries humor:
- **Pitch changes** mark incongruity
- **Stress patterns** highlight key words
- **Voice quality** (whisper, shout) signals frame shifts

**Our mapping:** delivery_archetype, modulator_range, pitch std (Louis: 6.83 st within quiet envelope)

## The performance-competence distinction

Attardo distinguishes:
- **Competence:** knowing what's funny (the text-level knowledge)
- **Performance:** making it funny in the moment (the delivery-level skill)

**Our mapping:**
- Competence = our gates (slop/edgy/feel) check the TEXT
- Performance = our delivery/timing/pause topics cover the DELIVERY

A joke can have good competence (well-structured) but bad performance (poor delivery), and vice versa.

## Audience as co-performer

Attardo emphasizes the audience is not passive:
- Laughter is a response that SHAPES the performance
- Comedians adjust timing based on audience reaction
- The "feedback loop" is essential

**Our mapping:** TAMS finding (audience impacts microtiming), the panel-show safety net (Lock), "stay in the bit" (Seinfeld advice to Louis).

## Gesture and physicality

Attardo discusses gesture as part of humor performance:
- Gestures can create incongruity (body contradicts words)
- Physical timing (Carlin: body-in-rhythm-with-voice)
- The "visual punchline" (Keaton, Chaplin)

**Our mapping:** PHYSICALITY-AXIS.md (voice-first vs mixed vs physical-first), TIC-TALK kinetic energy r=-0.75 with laughter

## Performance and social context

Attardo notes performance is shaped by:
- Venue (club vs theater vs TV)
- Culture (UK vs US rhythm differences)
- Genre (standup vs improv vs sketch)

**Our mapping:** region data, format types, the Lock CwE analysis (panel vs standup)
