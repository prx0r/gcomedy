## GOAT premise quotes (sourced, fair-use excerpts only)

**Chris Rock (HBO's Talking Funny, with Seinfeld, Louis CK, Gervais):**
> "A lot of comedians have great jokes, and they don't — like — 'Why isn't
> this working?' Because the audience does not understand the premise...
> If I set this premise up right, this joke will always work."
- Rock's fix: REPEAT the premise to keep the audience grounded (esp. long chunks).
- Matt Ruby's gloss: setup has to click — "like a string getting pulled in
  one of those old-timey talking dolls." If they're not onboard, even the
  best punchline can't save it — they're still figuring out the premise.
- Rock on writing about people: "Talk about what people DO, not what they ARE."

**Louis CK (same room):** regularly makes himself use his former closing bit
as a new opening bit — "just to fuck myself." Premise as self-sabotage;
forces the act to rebuild nightly. Also: "I try to make it seem like I'm
just getting this out, but I know all the moves, I know every little piece
of it" (GOLD.md transcript).

**Seinfeld:** tosses 10–20% of the act yearly, replaces with new jokes (vs
Rock/Louis writing a new hour yearly). Premise as perennial plant, not
annual rebuild.

**Gervais:** great comedy isn't being funny (some things are always funny)
— it's "going out and doing something no one else can do."

## Tetractys (the geometry spine, sourced)
- 1+2+3+4 = 10, the perfect number. Point → line → triangle → tetrahedron.
- Contains ALL concordant ratios: octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3 — every
  ratio expressible with the first four numbers (Stanford Encyclopedia;
  Cornford on the tetractys as "root and fountain of everflowing Nature").
- Pythagoreans swore oaths by it. Identified with cosmic harmony, the
  Sirens' song, the Delphic oracle ("What is the oracle at Delphi? The
  tetraktys, which is the harmony in which the Sirens sing").
- Harmony of the spheres: planets as harmonic instrument, orbits in the
  same numerical ratios as the scale (Proust 2009; Bessada 2014).
- Honesty note (SEP): hammer-weights discovery story is false (pitch isn't
  proportional to weight); verification traces to Hippasus/Philolaus, known
  possibly to Lasus. Don't tell the blacksmith story on screen.
