# BUTTON MICRO-TECHNIQUES — good vs bad, banked

> Sources: cadence theory, Carlin oratory closers, Seinfeld circular
> architecture, applause-curve analysis, Zeigarnik (production notes only).

## GOOD BUTTON vs BAD (the video's spine)

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Resolves trajectory (priority home, tension discharged) | Trails off ("anyway... so yeah") — trajectory left humming |
| Short (landing, not journey — under 15 words) | New material in the close (opens what it should shut) |
| Hands room its energy (applause is theirs) | Steps on the release (talks over the laugh) |
| Closes once, moves on | Double-button (close, reopen, close weaker) |
| Frees attention for the next bit | Leaks residue (next bit starts uphill) |

## Micro-technique bank

1. **Land, don't travel.** Final line under 15 words. Verdicts, not
   arguments. ("We're going away." — 3 words, whole set discharged.)
2. **Return-to-center check.** Last line's priority = the premise's
   priority. If the closer points somewhere new, it's an opener wearing
   a closer costume — move it.
3. **The applause handoff.** Stop one beat BEFORE the room peaks. Let the
   last 10% of the laugh belong to them with no words over it. Hands off.
4. **Circular close (Seinfeld).** Final bit reuses the first bit's premise,
   revoiced by the full hour. Trajectory literally returns to start
   coordinates with maximum archive. Strongest close architecture known.
5. **Verdict voice (Carlin).** Pitch rises, arms spread, oration register.
   Body closes the shape the words describe. Physical button under verbal one.
6. **Shrug button (Normand shape).** Throwaway last line, half-mumbled,
   pretending insignificance. Denies the trajectory while completing it.
   Opposite voicing, same geometry.
7. **False floor (advanced).** Close, then one reframe-line revealing the
   set was about something else. Two discharges, one line. Must be
   INSTANTLY legible or the room leaves confused. Save for closers.
8. **Early buttons compound.** Close EVERY bit, not just the set. Each
   closed bit frees its attention; five open bits = finale into 40%
   attention. Applause-curve proof on screen.
9. **No new worlds after 80%.** Past four-fifths, no new premises, no new
   characters, no new topics. Only returns and closures. New material late
   reads as stalling.
10. **Walk off on the peak.** Leave during applause, not after. The exit
    is part of the button — lingering leaks the discharge you just earned.
