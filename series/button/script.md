## [10:45] THE FALSE FLOOR

The advanced class: buttons that close one trajectory by opening a bigger one.

The room applauds the close, then — half a beat later — gasps as the reframe lands. Two discharges for one line: closure, then reopening at a higher level. Acaster's finales do this structurally (the identity crisis revealed as the subject all along); Louis does it topically (the bit was never about what it seemed). False floors are dangerous: mistime the second beat and the room leaves confused instead of expanded. The reframe must be INSTANTLY legible — one sentence, no setup of its own, riding entirely on the archive. When it works it's the closest comedy gets to the sublime: an ending that is also a beginning, a closed trajectory containing an open one.

## [11:30] FAILURE MODE

Two deaths, plus the four-stack they produce. One: the leaver — trailing off, "anyway, so yeah," next topic. The trajectory stays open; residual tension leaks into the next bit, which now starts uphill carrying someone else's unfinished business. Two: the double-button — closing, then reopening with "oh, one more thing." The second close never lands as hard (the room already discharged) and retroactively cheapens the first. Rule: close once, move on. The applause is the room's — leave it with them. Stacked underneath: the open-set autopsy — unclosed trajectory, new platform with no time to build, priority betrayal, apology disguised as gratitude. The button isn't the last joke. It's the discharge switch for everything before it.
