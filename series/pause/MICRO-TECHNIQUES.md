# PAUSE MICRO-TECHNIQUES — good vs bad, banked

> Sources: Twain (pause "exactly the right length"), TAMS 2026, DPV,
> Attardo/Pickering, Zhong 2026, 14x holds finding, Louis 8.16s, TAMS
> imaginary metronome.

## GOOD PAUSE vs BAD (the video's spine)

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Holds accrued weight (platform built first) | Dead air (nothing expected, nothing suspended) |
| 1.5–3.5s for big swings (asymmetric penalty: short −10%, long −40%) | Guessed duration (no calibration to the bit's weight) |
| Ends exactly on the turn (snap, not drift) | Leaks (slow fade back into speech, no edge) |
| Room leans in (visible stillness + micro-motion) | Room checks out (eyes drop, cough = curdled suspension) |
| Followed by shorter/faster delivery (snap contrast) | Followed by same pace (hold wasted, no trajectory) |

## Micro-technique bank

1. **Weigh before holding.** No hold without accrued expectation. Audit:
   what exactly is suspended? If nothing, it's dead air with confidence.
2. **The 1.5–3.5s window (big swings).** Under 1s for tags. Over 3.5s only
   with earned trust (Louis-tier room control). Calibrate per room nightly.
3. **Stillness, not stiffness.** Hold the body easy (Louis slump, not
   freeze). Tension lives in the room, not the shoulders. Stiffness reads
   as fear and the room smells it.
4. **End on the edge.** The return to speech must have an attack (consonant
   onset, pitch step, volume lift). Drifting back wastes the accrued weight.
5. **Twain's rule.** "Exactly the right length — no more and no less."
   Rehearse holds with a stopwatch once, then by feel forever. The stopwatch
   builds the feel; feel replaces the watch.
6. **Hold-then-snap trajectory.** Pause 1.4–2× baseline, delivery 1.48×
   after. Two phases, one gesture (fermata → rush). Practice as a single
   motion, not pause-then-speech.
7. **Never step on laughter.** After the laugh starts, hands off until it
   crests. Talking over release steals the room's half of the event.
8. **Dead-air test.** Record the set, mute the voice, listen to silences
   alone. Holds sound full (room noise, leaning). Dead air sounds empty
   (coughs, chairs). Mix accordingly in rewrite.
9. **TAMS metronome.** Hold inter-onset intervals steady across a tour
   (same silences nightly to 0.1s). Composed, not improvised. Fix the
   holds in rehearsal; let laughs vary.
10. **Spend holds like money.** ~2 big holds per set max. Third big hold
    bankrupts the currency (room stops lending attention). Budget silence
    the way drummers budget fills.
