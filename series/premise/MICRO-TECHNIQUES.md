# PREMISE MICRO-TECHNIQUES — good vs bad, banked

> Sources: Rock Talking Funny (exact quote), Acaster red-dress case,
> Seinfeld noticing, Gervais territory claims, Johnstone Impro.

## GOOD PREMISE vs BAD (the video's spine)

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| One world, declared fast (≤25 words) | Three half-worlds, none committed (hedged premise) |
| Attitude attached (comic's take present) | Neutral topic dump (facts with no vector) |
| Room can co-build instantly (shared references) | Requires homework (unshared context, in-jokes) |
| Stable enough to violate (holds still) | Shifts mid-bit (rules change to force jokes) |
| Re-stated mid-chunk (campfire fed) | Stated once, assumed forever (decays by minute 3) |

## Micro-technique bank

1. **Rock's rule, literal.** "If I set this premise up right, this joke
   will always work." Test every failing bit: was the premise understood?
   9 times in 10, no. Fix the platform, not the punch.
2. **Attitude, not topic (Gervais).** Airports-as-purgatory vs airports-as-
   playground: same topic, opposite worlds. The premise was never the
   situation — it's the take installed as shared priority.
3. **Red-dress proof (Acaster).** True story rejected, fiction bought.
   Belief irrelevant; co-signing is everything. If they buy in, fiction
   outperforms fact.
4. **Seinfeld noticing.** Premise fuel = noticed detail (hinges, scarring,
   dread). Train the noticing muscle: 3 observations/day, no jokes
   attached. Premises stockpile; jokes withdraw.
5. **Repeat the premise (Rock's fix).** Restate mid-chunk in new words.
   Campfire rule. Demo same chunk with/without restatement.
6. **Buried vs engineered (Louis vs Seinfeld).** Smuggle inside story OR
   assemble with visible precision. Pick per bit; never half-bury (worst
   of both: slow AND unclear).
7. **Premise theft (road craft).** Point at the room's pre-loaded world
   (city, traffic, team losing). Zero beats, full platform. Masters squat;
   beginners build.
8. **Offer, don't impose (Johnstone).** Present the world as invitation
   ("imagine if..."), not decree. Rooms enter offered worlds, resist
   imposed ones.
9. **One attitude per bit.** Two takes = two worlds = room splits. Save
   the second take for its own bit.
10. **Premise clarity drill.** State any bit's premise in ≤10 words. Can't
    = no premise (a pile of jokes, not a bit). Do it for every bit in
    the set, weekly.
