## THESIS

**The target decides the safety math — who you attack determines whether
the room laughs, groans, or complains.** Self, powerful, species. Never
victim. Targeting does three jobs: names the butt first, matches assumed
to intended, holds one butt per joke.

Reservoir (≤10, frozen after halfway): target, butt, self, powerful,
species, victim, veto, aim, liability, ledger.

## THEORY (production notes only — never narrated)

- **Socratopia Ch.18 "Reading the Room" (fetched full text, socratopia.app,
  2026).** Definition: the *assumed target* is who the listener takes the
  joke to be on, decided from setup subject-position + persona "at the
  speed the rest of the assumption is formed." Direction sets the price of
  agreement: at the powerful = free; at oneself = free if the room believes
  you're safe; at the shared situation = cheapest; at the vulnerable =
  visible agreement with the stronger side, with an audible hesitation.
  Repairs: replant the setup (Move 1), rule of three (Move 2), end on the
  replacing word (Move 3). Crowd-work codicil (Ch.15): "the person is not
  the target, the room is" — including vs using. This chapter IS the case
  beat's machinery.
- **jokegate TARGETS + THE-LINE (repo: EDGY.md).** Crossing formula:
  `line_crossed ⟺ violation + distance > threshold` subject to
  `target ∈ {powerful, self, species}`, `target ≠ real_victim`, benign
  present. Four lines: HARM (butt ≠ victim), STAKE, IDENTIFICATION,
  DISTANCE (master dial; at 1.0 distance itself is the benign-maker).
  Target hierarchy: powerful > self > species > never victim. Self-target
  bonus in edge scoring; confessional +0.2 (working threshold, CALIBRATE).
- **McGraw & Warren 2010 (fetched, petermcgraw.org).** Distance from the
  violation as benign-maker — the lab version of "punch up": powerful
  targets are *psychologically distant*, victims are near. The essay's
  "safety math" is this finding in room language.
- **comedywriter.info target-status (search-verified excerpts; fetch 403).**
  "Always punch up, attack institutions not victims"; dark-comedy test:
  "would I say this in front of someone directly affected?" Target
  hierarchy in descending safety — matches jokegate ordering.

## GENESIS

Rock retires his most famous routine. In the 2005 60 Minutes interview he
says he has never done "Niggas vs Black People" again and probably never
will — "some people that were racist thought they had license to say
nigger, so I'm done with that routine" (Wikipedia, verified 2026). The
bit didn't change; the *room's assignment* changed — the assumed butt
drifted from the behavior to the people, and the author pulled the bit
rather than re-aim it at a safer chair. That is the victim-veto as a
career event: the strongest possible evidence that target is a property
of the room-comic contract, not of the text. (Status genesis, Ep.11: the
same routine *earned* its rank in clubs; Target genesis: the same routine
*lost* its license in culture.)

## SUBJECT-QUOTE

Jimmy Carr, in the T4 compilation (official channel, transcript verified):
*"I don't know where the mark is until I overstep it."* The butt-ledger
philosophy from the comic most famous for testing the veto — stated as
method, not apology. Essay uses it to open the FAILURE beat: the mark is
found by stepping, and the ledger is what you write down after.

## UNIVERSAL

Every joke has a butt — and so does every argument, roast, review, and
family story. Naming the butt is honesty: it says who pays for the laugh
before the laugh is spent. The video closes outward: people who name their
targets in comedy start noticing unnamed targets everywhere else.
