# TAGS: HITTING IT AGAIN — full script

> Video 4, Fundamentals of Comedy. ~18 min. Thesis: A tag spends the
> punch's leftover energy before it decays — same broken world, second
> fracture, smaller hammer.

---

**[0:00] ENGINE NAMED.** The punch broke the world. The room is laughing.
You have about four seconds before the laugh decays and the world seals
itself back up. A tag is what you do in those four seconds: hit it again.
Same world, new fracture, smaller hammer. Tags are the highest-ROI words
in comedy — the punch did all the expensive setup work, and the tag spends
the change.

**[0:30] PROOF.** Louis Monopoly: the punch lands ("give it all to me"),
and then — "you're going down hard. It's really bad." Two tags. Same
broken world (ruthless father, crying child), second and third fractures,
each shorter than the last. [CLIP] The tags get 70% of the punch's laugh
for 10% of the words. That's the economics that makes tags mandatory:
nothing else in comedy pays that rate.

**[1:30] THESIS + VOCABULARY.** A tag does three things: it assumes the
punch's world (no rebuilding, no re-explaining), it finds an unfractured
corner of it, and it fractures that corner faster than the first break.
New term, one only: decay window — the ~4 seconds of laugh-energy during
which tags live. After it closes, you're back to setup economics.

**[2:30] LENS (music first).** The encore riff. A guitarist lands the big
bend, the crowd roars, and instead of stopping he squeezes two more bent
notes out of the same string position — same fret, same string, new cries.
Nobody applauds the encore riff as composition. They applaud it as nerve:
he stayed up there. Tags are encore riffs in meaning-space. Same position
(the broken world), new cries (new fractures), nerve over architecture.

**[5:00] PERSONA.** Normand: the machine-gun tagger — tags stacked so fast
they overlap the laugh, each one shorter, rhythm doing half the work. Louis:
the excavator — tags that dig (the second tag darker than the punch, the
rule being you can go darker once the world is already broken because the
benign frame is fully installed). Same function (spend the decay window),
opposite risk profiles (speed vs depth).

**[8:00] CASE STUDY.** Tag chain dissected. [CLIP: 1 punch + 3 tags] On
screen: word counts descending (punch 9 words, tag1 6, tag2 4, tag3 3 —
typical decay curve). Laugh waveform under each: punch laugh longest,
tags progressively shorter but DENSER (laughs per word rises even as total
laugh falls — the ROI curve). Then the fourth tag that shouldn't exist:
show a tag chain pushed one past its decay window — watch the room go
polite. The window is real and it closes.

**[13:00] SECOND SPECIMEN.** Improv tag-run: same game, new instances,
no script. [CLIP] Different handling — written tags are engineered
fractures, improv tags are discovered ones — but the decay economics are
identical. The improviser feels the window closing in their body and
either lands the tag or edits the scene. That feeling has a name in the
data: it's the laugh-decay slope, and experienced players surf it the way
surfers read waves.

**[16:00] FAILURE MODE.** Two deaths. One: theExplainer tag — restating
the punch instead of fracturing anew. Zero new fracture, zero laugh,
and it spends the decay window on nothing. Two: the fourth-tag overreach —
pushing past the window because the third tag worked. The room's laughter
was already decaying; the extra tag doesn't extend it, it exposes the
machinery. Rule: tags diminish or die. Never tag sideways (new world =
new setup economics, and you're spending setup money you don't have).

**[18:00] UNIVERSAL + CLOSE.** Everything good has tags. Conversations:
the follow-up line that's funnier than the story. Arguments: the second
example that ends it. Music: the encore riff. Tags are what you do with
leftover energy before it decays — in comedy and everywhere else. Next
week: what comes back months later. Callbacks — time travel for comedians.
