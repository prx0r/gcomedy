# JOKE (EPISODE 0) MICRO-TECHNIQUES — good vs bad, banked

> Sources: Wikipedia ladder, Laffstr glossary, Dean, Twain taxonomy,
> GTVH joke definition (setup + punch, two scripts opposed).

## GOOD JOKE vs BAD (the episode's spine)

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| One setup, one punch, one laugh point | Two punches fighting (split attention, half laughs) |
| Setup builds a complete false world | Setup hedges ("kinda, sorta, almost") |
| Punch lands on the final word | Punch word buried, explanation follows |
| Self-contained (works cold, no context) | Needs footnotes (callback without plant, in-joke) |
| 5–30 seconds (atomic weight) | Bloated to bit-length without bit architecture |

## Micro-technique bank

1. **One laugh point.** Count them: exactly one per joke. Two laugh points
   = two jokes sharing a setup (split them) or a tag hiding (label it).
2. **Complete false world.** Setup must stand alone as believable BEFORE
   the punch. Test: cover the punch — is the setup interesting alone? No =
   no platform.
3. **Final-word landing.** Funnest word last, full stop after. No trailing
   prepositions, no "you know?", no laughter-cushion.
4. **Cold-open test.** Tell it to a stranger with zero context. Works =
   joke. Needs backstory = bit fragment, not a joke.
5. **Twain sort.** Humorous (manner-dependent, needs YOUR delivery) vs
   comic (matter-dependent, works on paper). Know which yours is; don't
   submit a manner-joke to a writing contest or a matter-joke to a
   deadpan contest.
6. **Paraprosdokian check.** Does the final clause force reprocessing of
   everything before? If yes, structure is sound regardless of topic.
7. **Setup/punch ratio audit.** Atomic jokes run 3:1 to 5:1 (setup:punch
   words). Richer = bit territory. Leaner = one-liner territory.
8. **Kill your darlings (setup trim).** Remove one setup sentence. Still
   works? Remove another. Stop at the last removal before death — that's
   the joke's true size.
9. **No-telegraph scan.** Can the room finish your sentence? If yes,
   rewrite setup (hide path) or punch (new destination).
10. **The stranger encore.** Best test in comedy: new room, cold, first
    30 seconds. Jokes that work cold are atomic. Jokes needing warmth
    are bits waiting for architecture.
