# DELIVERY MICRO-TECHNIQUES — good vs bad, banked

## GOOD DELIVERY vs BAD
| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Energy = presence (room feels held) | Energy = volume/pace (louder/faster, still empty) |
| Commit 100% to act-outs (vulnerability ask honored) | Half-commit (room senses the hedge, checks out) |
| Mirror-rehearsed faces/body (dozens of reps) | First-time body (face the mirror never saw) |
| Slow enough to let jokes land | Rushing past laughs (stepping on release) |
| Mic still, hands free (technique invisible) | Mic switching, finger on grille (distraction tax) |

## Bank
1. **Presence, not volume (iamgarron).** Energy is being there, not loudness. Fix: record one set, watch with sound off — presence visible?
2. **100% commit (Mordkillius/SnarkAnthony).** Act-outs ask the room to suspend disbelief (bigger ask). Go all in or cut it.
3. **Mirror hours (TheLateThagSimmons).** Dozens of faces at home before one on stage. "Natural" is rehearsed.
4. **Slow down (consensus first-set note).** Let jokes land before next setup. Rushing reads as fear.
5. **Mic discipline.** Still mic, free hands. Every fidget costs attention.
6. **Space the stage (EmrysSmith).** Body zones per character; head-turns shrink as scene establishes. Minimum transition words.
7. **Quick cuts (iamgarron).** Face different directions per character; distinct voices optional, blocking mandatory.
8. **Return energy-matched (crowd-work rule).** Come back to material at riff energy, then ratchet down deliberately.
9. **Dry-run physical tags.** Physical bits need MORE rehearsal than verbal (failure is visible, not just audible).
10. **Bad-show autopsy.** Watch tape at 2x: every fidget, rush, hedge timestamps itself. Fix one per week.
