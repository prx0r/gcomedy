# SERIES TROPES — reusable segments in every video

> Same segment shapes, different content. Viewers learn the shapes in
> Episode 0; every later video reuses them so attention goes to content,
> never to figuring out the format.

## 1. COLD OPEN (0:00–0:45)
Black → one-line thesis as a dare → one clip proving it before explanation.
Never introduce, never recap. (Ep 0: "What is a joke? Your life.")

## 2. COLOR SWITCH (the grammar demo)
Same joke twice: first raw, then with blue/red subtitles switching at the
turn. Used once per video minimum — always on new viewers' first encounter
with that video's function.

## 3. AUTOPSY (mid-video, ~2 min)
Freeze one beat mid-flight and take it apart: word counts, pause timings,
assumption shifts (who/what/where/when/why/how explicit vs implicit).
Signature segment; the "how it works" payload.

## 4. CLOCK (timing proof)
Pause timer + waveform + laugh markers on one hold. Timer runs, tension
bar fills, pop frame at laugh onset. Used for every timing claim.

## 5. SPLIT COMPARE (good vs bad)
Same premise, two handlings, side by side: good version vs dead version.
Only difference named afterward. (Setup: platform vs no platform. Punch:
short vs explained. Tags: diminishing vs flat. Etc.)

## 6. THE LADDER (escalation/tag videos)
Intensity curve on screen, rising per item, break marked red. Reused shape
for rule-of-three, tag stacks, callback stacks.

## 7. RETURN ARC (callback video + series coda)
Arc leaving present position, landing on earlier marker lighting gold.
Reused for episode-level callbacks (mother joke returns) and series-level
(episode 8 returns to episode 0's black).

## 8. GLOSSARY CARD (once per new term)
Term + one-line definition + 3-second example. Cards persist in video
description. Never define twice across the series.

## 9. HOOK (last 60s)
Next video's question stated as dare, not teaser. ("A platform is only
good for standing on. Next week: how to climb without falling off.")
